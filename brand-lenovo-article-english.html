Who is Lenovo?

Lenovo is a Chinese multinational company specializing in information technology. Founded in 1984 in Beijing under the name Legend Holdings by Liu Chuanzhi and eleven other engineers from the Chinese Academy of Sciences, the company adopted the name Lenovo in 2003 — a combination of "Le-" (from Legend) and "novo" (Latin for "new").

The defining event that propelled Lenovo onto the global stage was the acquisition, in 2005, of IBM's PC division for $1.75 billion. This deal allowed it to acquire the legendary ThinkPad and ThinkCentre brands, as well as a global distribution network and a reputation for unmatched reliability in the professional world.

In 2014, Lenovo reached another milestone by acquiring IBM’s x86 server division and Motorola Mobility from Google, expanding its portfolio to include smartphones and data center infrastructure. Today, Lenovo is the world’s leading PC manufacturer by volume, with a market share regularly exceeding 24%, ahead of HP and Dell.

Its global headquarters is located in Beijing (China), with a second operational headquarters in Morrisville (North Carolina, United States). The company employs more than 77,000 people worldwide and sells its products in over 180 countries.

Key history 📅

Major milestones of Lenovo

1984

Founding of Legend Holdings in Beijing by Liu Chuanzhi and eleven engineers, with an initial capital of 200,000 yuan.

1994

IPO on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange. Legend becomes China’s leading PC manufacturer.

2003

The company is renamed Lenovo, a contraction of "Legend" and "novo" (Latin for new), to support its international expansion.

2005

Acquisition of IBM’s PC division for $1.75 billion. Lenovo gains ThinkPad, ThinkCentre brands and a global network.

2011

Lenovo becomes the world’s second-largest PC manufacturer, surpassing Dell for the first time.

2013

Lenovo reaches the #1 global PC manufacturer position, overtaking HP.

2014

Two major strategic acquisitions: IBM’s x86 server division ($2.1B) and Motorola Mobility from Google ($2.9B).

2020–2024

Lenovo strengthens its global PC leadership and expands into AI solutions, edge computing, and high-performance servers through its Infrastructure Solutions Group (ISG).

What Lenovo Makes 🏭

Lenovo covers a very broad range of products, structured into four major business groups: IDG (Intelligent Devices Group) for consumers and professionals, ISG (Infrastructure Solutions Group) for servers and cloud, SSG (Solutions & Services Group) for managed services, and MBG (Mobile Business Group) for Motorola smartphones.

Focus — Monitors

Lenovo Monitor Offering 🖱️

Lenovo markets its displays mainly under the ThinkVision brand, with product lines covering professional, creative, and consumer use cases. The brand is less well-known than Dell UltraSharp in this segment, but offers high-quality products with modern connectivity.

  • 🎨

    ThinkVision — P Series (Creator)

    Lenovo’s high-end displays designed for visual professionals. The P Series stands out with wide color gamut coverage (DCI-P3, Adobe RGB), factory calibration, IPS or OLED panels, and resolutions up to 4K. Available in 27 and 32 inches, they include a USB-C hub with up to 90W Power Delivery, allowing you to connect and charge a laptop with a single cable.

    ThinkVision — T Series (Professional)

    The reference range for businesses and enterprise environments. T Series monitors offer full ergonomics (height, tilt, swivel, pivot), professional connectivity (DisplayPort, HDMI, USB-A, USB-C), and IPS panels for good viewing angles. Discreet and durable, they are built for intensive office use. Available from 22 to 27 inches in Full HD, QHD, and 4K.

    Covered here

    ThinkVision — E Series (Essential)

    Lenovo’s entry-level professional lineup, designed for tight budgets without sacrificing the essentials. Decent IPS panels, basic connectivity (HDMI + VGA or DisplayPort), and height-adjustable stands depending on the model. Ideal for secondary office setups, meeting rooms, or non-profit organizations. Available in 21.5 to 24 inches in Full HD.

Summary

Lenovo in a few words 💡

In forty years, Lenovo has grown from a Beijing startup founded in an apartment into the world’s number one personal computing company. Its defining move remains the acquisition of IBM’s PC division in 2005, which instantly provided the prestige, global network, and credibility it needed to conquer Western markets.

Lenovo’s strength lies in the clarity of its segmentation: ThinkPad for professionals, Yoga for creatives, Legion for gamers, and IdeaPad for the general public. Each lineup has a strong identity and a clearly defined audience, avoiding unnecessary overlap.

With its shift toward embedded AI solutions, “AI-ready” PCs (with integrated NPUs), and a rapidly growing server business through ISG, Lenovo is positioning itself to remain a key player in a rapidly evolving market.
