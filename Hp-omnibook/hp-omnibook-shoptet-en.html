💻 HP Laptops

HP OmniBook — The HP laptop built for the AI era

Launched in 2024, the HP OmniBook lineup marks the return of a historic name at HP — and above all the arrival of a new generation of laptops designed around built-in artificial intelligence. Processors with integrated NPU, Microsoft Copilot+ compatibility, ARM or x86 depending on the model: the OmniBook is HP’s vision of what the laptop of tomorrow will be.

🔮 Lineup Overview

The HP OmniBook is an entirely new lineup launched by HP to respond to the rise of Copilot+ PCs introduced by Microsoft in 2024. These machines include a NPU (Neural Processing Unit) — a chip dedicated to processing artificial intelligence locally, directly on the device, without relying on the cloud.

The OmniBook lineup is available in two distinct architectures: models based on Snapdragon X Elite / X Plus chips from Qualcomm (ARM architecture, excellent battery life) and models equipped with Intel Core Ultra 200 processors (x86 architecture, better software compatibility). Both offer significantly improved local AI acceleration compared to previous generations.

In practice, the OmniBook enables features such as real-time transcription, advanced noise cancellation, local image generation via Windows Studio Effects, and enhanced performance in AI-compatible applications (Adobe, DaVinci Resolve, Microsoft 365). It is available in 14 and 16 inches, with premium aluminum finishes.

👍 Pros & Cons

The HP OmniBook is a young and innovative lineup — here are its current strengths and limitations.

✔ Pros
  • Integrated NPU — local AI acceleration without relying on the cloud
  • Copilot+ compatible — access to advanced AI features in Windows 11
  • Outstanding battery life on Snapdragon models (up to 20 hours)
  • Near-instant boot and immediate wake from sleep
  • Premium aluminum finish — modern and lightweight design
  • Built-in real-time transcription and advanced noise cancellation
  • Local AI processing — data is not sent over the internet
  • IPS or OLED display depending on configuration
  • USB-C fast charging — convenient for mobility
✘ Cons
  • Very recent lineup — limited long-term reliability feedback
  • Snapdragon models: limited compatibility with some legacy x86 software
  • AI features still evolving — some promises not fully delivered yet
  • High price for a lineup without an established track record
  • No dedicated GPU — not suitable for gaming or intensive 3D work
  • Limited connectivity — few ports, adapter often required
  • Soldered RAM and not upgradeable on most configurations
  • AI application ecosystem still limited outside Microsoft 365
💡 Note: Copilot+ AI features such as Recall (visual activity history) raise privacy concerns. It is recommended to check which features are enabled and configure your privacy settings after purchase.

📋 Main versions of the lineup

HP OmniBook X 14 — Snapdragon X Elite / X Plus

The flagship model of the lineup, powered by Qualcomm’s Snapdragon X Elite chip. ARM architecture, up to 20 hours of battery life, instant startup, and optional 5G connectivity. Ideal for mobile professionals who want maximum battery life with built-in AI features. Make sure to check software compatibility before purchasing.

HP OmniBook Ultra 14 — Intel Core Ultra 200

The x86 version of the OmniBook, featuring an Intel Core Ultra 200 Series processor. Better software compatibility than the Snapdragon version, while still benefiting from Intel’s NPU for Copilot+ features. Slightly lower battery life (12 to 15 hours advertised), but more versatile for users relying on traditional Windows software.

HP OmniBook 16 — Large-format AI laptop

The 16-inch version of the lineup, designed for users who prefer a larger screen. It includes the same AI technologies as the 14-inch models, with a bigger display and improved thermal management. Heavier and less portable, but offers a more comfortable workspace for intensive office work and video meetings.

✅ Our opinion

The HP OmniBook is a bold move by HP toward the future of PCs. It represents a real technological shift with the integration of NPUs and Copilot+ features, all within a well-designed chassis and impressive battery life on Snapdragon models.

However, the lineup is still very new, and the Windows AI ecosystem is still evolving. If you are not in a hurry, waiting for the second generation may provide more mature features. If you want to stay ahead of the curve now, the OmniBook Ultra 14 (Intel) is the safer choice due to its superior software compatibility.
