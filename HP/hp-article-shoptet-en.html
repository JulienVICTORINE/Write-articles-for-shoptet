Hewlett-Packard — HP

Founded in a Palo Alto garage in 1939, HP is one of the oldest technology companies in the world and one of the largest manufacturers of PCs and printers on the planet.

Overview

Who is HP? 🏢

Hewlett-Packard — better known as HP — is an American company founded in 1939 by Bill Hewlett and Dave Packard in the famous garage of Packard’s home in Palo Alto, California. This garage is now considered the birthplace of Silicon Valley. Their first commercial product was an audio oscillator (the HP 200A), notably sold to Walt Disney Studios for the post-production of Fantasia.

For decades, HP established itself as a pioneer in measurement instruments, scientific calculators, laser printers, and personal computers. In 2002, HP merged with Compaq for $25 billion, becoming at the time the world's leading PC manufacturer and significantly strengthening its server and IT services offerings.

In 2015, the company underwent a historic split into two separate entities: HP Inc., which retained the PC and printer businesses, and Hewlett Packard Enterprise (HPE), which took over servers, storage, networking, and enterprise services. HP Inc. is the focus of this article.

Today, HP Inc. is the second or third largest PC manufacturer in the world, depending on the quarter, with a market share ranging between 20% and 22%, behind Lenovo and closely competing with Dell. HP Inc. is headquartered in Palo Alto, California, and the brand is present in more than 170 countries.

Key milestones 📅

Major milestones in HP’s history

1939

Bill Hewlett and Dave Packard founded HP in a Palo Alto garage with $538 in capital. Their first product: the HP 200A audio oscillator, sold to Walt Disney.

1972

HP launched the HP-35, the world’s first handheld scientific calculator, revolutionizing engineering and research.

1984

Introduction of the HP LaserJet, the first mass-market laser printer. It redefined office printing and has remained a core pillar of HP’s business for 40 years.

1994

Launch of the HP Pavilion, a consumer PC line that became one of the best-selling in the world throughout the 1990s and 2000s.

2002

Merger with Compaq for $25 billion. HP acquired the iPAQ and Compaq Presario lines and a dominant position in the x86 server market.

2006

HP surpassed Dell and became the world’s leading PC manufacturer, a position it held until 2013 when Lenovo overtook it.

2012

HP acquired Autonomy for $11 billion — a deal that resulted in an $8.8 billion write-down and a major accounting scandal.

2015

Split into two independent entities: HP Inc. (PCs and printers) and Hewlett Packard Enterprise (servers, storage, services). Meg Whitman led HPE, while Dion Weisler headed HP Inc.

2020

HP Inc. weathered the pandemic thanks to a surge in PC demand driven by remote work and online education. Chromebook demand skyrocketed in the education sector.

2023–2024

HP refocused its strategy on AI PCs (with integrated NPU processors such as Intel Core Ultra / AMD Ryzen AI), hardware-based cybersecurity (HP Wolf Security), and sustainable printing solutions.

Product Range

What HP makes 🏭

HP Inc. structures its offering around two main pillars: personal systems (laptops, desktops, workstations, monitors) and printing solutions (inkjet, laser, large-format printers). The brand also covers accessories, hardware security, and managed services.

💻

Laptops

From entry-level Chromebooks to the premium Spectre x360, including professional EliteBooks and OMEN gaming laptops — HP covers every market segment.

🖥️

Desktops

From ProDesk and EliteDesk for businesses to consumer OmniDesk models, as well as Z-series workstations for creatives and engineers.

🖱️

Monitors

E-Series displays for office use, Z-Series for creatives and workstations, and OMEN for gaming — with sizes ranging from 21 to 40 inches.

🖨️

Printers & Printing

HP is the global leader in printing. Product lines include LaserJet (pro laser), DeskJet and ENVY (consumer inkjet), OfficeJet Pro (SMBs), DesignJet (large format), and HP Instant Ink (subscription service).

🔒

Security — HP Wolf Security

A hardware-based cybersecurity suite integrated into HP business PCs: firmware-level malware protection, threat isolation via micro-virtualization, and endpoint risk management.

🗄️

Servers — HPE (separate entity)

Since 2015, servers have been managed by Hewlett Packard Enterprise (HPE), an independent entity. The ProLiant, Synergy, and Apollo lines cover enterprise datacenter needs.

⌨️

Peripherals & Accessories

Keyboards, mice, webcams, Thunderbolt docks, headsets, and bags — HP offers a complete ecosystem of accessories designed to match its PC lineup.

🎮

Gaming — OMEN & Victus

Two gaming brands: OMEN for high-end (RTX 4090 GPUs, 240 Hz displays, OMEN Hub) and Victus for accessible gaming with strong performance at a more affordable price.

Summary

HP in a nutshell

Over 85 years, HP has gone through every major tech revolution — from oscilloscopes to scientific calculators, from laser printers to premium laptops, from datacenter servers to AI PCs. Born in a Palo Alto garage, the company became a symbol of American tech and the birthplace of what would later be called Silicon Valley.

HP Inc.’s strength lies in the clarity of its segmentation: Spectre for premium, Envy for stylish mid-range, EliteBook for secure business, OMEN for high-end gaming, Victus for accessible gaming, OmniBook for AI — each lineup has a clear identity and audience.

With its shift toward Copilot+ PCs, hardware-based security through HP Wolf, and AI integration into workflows, HP is positioning itself to remain a key player in a rapidly evolving market.
