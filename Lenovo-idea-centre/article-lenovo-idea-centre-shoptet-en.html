The IdeaCentre range from offers desktop computers designed for the general public: compact, affordable, and increasingly AI-oriented. Here’s an overview of this lineup, which ranges from discreet mini PCs to elegant all-in-one systems.

Overview of the range

The IdeaCentre includes several desktop formats designed for home or family use. There are mainly three variants: the Tower (IdeaCentre Tower), for those who need power with upgradable components; the Mini (IdeaCentre Mini), a compact PC of about 1 liter that is very discreet on a desk; and the All-in-One (IdeaCentre AIO), which integrates the screen and system unit into a single chassis.

Since 2025, Lenovo has gradually introduced AI features into this range, notably through Intel Core Ultra or Qualcomm Snapdragon X Plus processors, with integrated NPUs capable of accelerating intelligent tasks locally, without relying on the cloud.

Advantages and disadvantages

Pros

  • Wide range of formats (tower, mini, AIO)
  • Affordable entry-level pricing
  • Clean and discreet design
  • Integrated AI features on recent generations
  • Mini and Tower are upgradeable (RAM, storage)
  • Good Lenovo after-sales reputation

Cons

  • Not suitable for serious gaming
  • Fan noise under load (especially Mini)
  • AIO is not easy to repair
  • No dedicated GPU in entry-level models
  • Preinstalled software sometimes unnecessary

Our opinion

8/10
Great choice for family use Good value for money

The IdeaCentre range delivers on its promise: offering a reliable, well-built, and affordable desktop for everyday use. The Mini stands out for its compactness, the Tower for its versatility, and the AIO for its elegance on the desk.

It’s not the most exciting lineup on the market, but it is a safe choice for those looking for a hassle-free desktop, backed by a solid brand. With the growing integration of AI in newer generations, Lenovo positions the IdeaCentre to remain relevant in the years ahead.
