Overview

Who is Lenovo?

Lenovo is a Chinese multinational company specializing in information technology. It was founded in 1984 in Beijing under the name Legend Holdings by Liu Chuanzhi and eleven other engineers from the Chinese Academy of Sciences. In 2003, the company adopted the name Lenovo — a combination of "Le-" (from Legend) and "novo" (Latin for “new”).

The key moment that propelled Lenovo onto the global stage was the acquisition of IBM’s PC division in 2005 for $1.75 billion. This gave it access to iconic brands like ThinkPad and ThinkCentre, along with a global distribution network and a strong reputation for reliability in professional environments.

In 2014, Lenovo took another major step by acquiring IBM’s x86 server division and Motorola Mobility from Google, expanding its portfolio into smartphones and data center infrastructure. Today, Lenovo is the world’s largest PC manufacturer by volume, with a market share regularly exceeding 24%, ahead of HP and Dell.

The company’s global headquarters are located in Beijing (China), with a second operational headquarters in Morrisville (North Carolina, USA). Lenovo employs more than 77,000 people worldwide and sells its products in over 180 countries.

Key history 📅

Major milestones

1984

Founded as Legend Holdings in Beijing by Liu Chuanzhi and eleven engineers, with an initial capital of 200,000 yuan.

1994

IPO on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange. Legend becomes the leading PC manufacturer in China.

2003

The company is renamed Lenovo, a combination of "Legend" and "novo" (Latin for "new"), to prepare for international expansion.

2005

Acquisition of IBM’s PC division for $1.75 billion. Lenovo inherits the ThinkPad and ThinkCentre brands along with a global network.

2011

Lenovo becomes the second largest PC manufacturer in the world, surpassing Dell for the first time.

2013

Lenovo reaches the number one position worldwide among PC manufacturers, overtaking HP.

2014

Double strategic acquisition: IBM’s x86 server division ($2.1B) and Motorola Mobility from Google ($2.9B).

2020–2024

Lenovo strengthens its global leadership in the PC market and expands into AI solutions, edge computing, and high-performance servers through its Infrastructure Solutions Group (ISG).

Product range

What Lenovo makes 🏭

Lenovo covers a very broad range of products, structured into four main business groups: IDG (Intelligent Devices Group) for consumers and professionals, ISG (Infrastructure Solutions Group) for servers and cloud, SSG (Solutions & Services Group) for managed services, and MBG (Mobile Business Group) for Motorola smartphones.

Focus — Desktop PCs

Lenovo offers a complete range of desktop systems, from compact business mini PCs to ultra-powerful workstations for creative and scientific professionals.

Available in tower, mini, micro, and all-in-one formats, ThinkCentre stands out for its ease of deployment, durability, and management tools (Lenovo Device Manager, SCCM). Focus — Monitors

Lenovo monitor lineup 🖱️

Lenovo primarily markets its displays under the ThinkVision brand, with ranges covering professional, creative, and consumer use. The brand is less well-known than Dell UltraSharp in this segment but offers high-quality products with modern connectivity.

  • 🎨

    ThinkVision — P Series (Creator)

    Lenovo’s high-end displays designed for visual professionals. The P Series stands out with wide color gamut coverage (DCI-P3, Adobe RGB), factory calibration, IPS or OLED panels, and resolutions up to 4K. Available in 27 and 32 inches, they include a USB-C hub with up to 90W Power Delivery, allowing you to connect and charge a laptop with a single cable.

    Covered here
  • 🏢

    ThinkVision — T Series (Professional)

    The reference range for businesses and IT fleets. T Series displays offer full ergonomics (height, tilt, swivel, pivot), professional connectivity (DisplayPort, HDMI, USB-A, USB-C), and IPS panels for wide viewing angles. Discreet and durable, they are built for intensive use in office environments. Available from 22 to 27 inches in Full HD, QHD, and 4K.

    Covered here

    ThinkVision — E Series (Essential)

    Lenovo’s entry-level professional range, designed for tight budgets without sacrificing essentials. Decent IPS panels, basic connectivity (HDMI + VGA or DisplayPort), and height-adjustable stands depending on the model. Ideal for secondary office setups, meeting rooms, or non-profits. Available from 21.5 to 24 inches in Full HD.

In summary

Lenovo in a nutshell 💡

In forty years, Lenovo has grown from a Beijing startup founded in an apartment into the world's number one player in personal computing. Its masterstroke remains the acquisition of IBM’s PC division in 2005, which instantly gave it the prestige, network, and global credibility it needed to conquer Western markets.

Lenovo’s strength lies in the clarity of its segmentation: ThinkPad for professionals, Yoga for creatives, Legion for gamers, and IdeaPad for general consumers. Each range has a strong identity and a clearly defined target, avoiding unnecessary overlaps.

With its shift toward embedded AI solutions, "AI-ready" PCs (with integrated NPU processors), and a rapidly growing server business through ISG, Lenovo is positioning itself to remain a key player in a rapidly evolving market.
